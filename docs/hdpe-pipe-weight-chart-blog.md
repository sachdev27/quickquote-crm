## HDPE Pipe Weight Chart, Explained Simply

If you have ever asked a pipe supplier for a quote, you have probably heard words like *PE100*, *PN 10*, *SDR 13.6* or "110 mm". They sound technical, but they are just labels. Once you know what each one means, you can read any HDPE pipe chart in less than a minute, and you can work out how heavy your order will be before it arrives.

This guide explains the weight chart in plain language, with real examples and the full chart for the common sizes.

## The 30-second version

- **Size (OD)** is the outside diameter of the pipe in millimetres, for example 63 mm or 110 mm.
- **PE63 / PE80 / PE100** is the strength grade of the plastic. A higher number means a stronger plastic.
- **PN** is the pressure the pipe is made to carry, in bar. PN 10 means 10 bar, which is about 100 metres of water head.
- **SDR** tells you how thick the wall is compared with the size. A **smaller SDR means a thicker wall**.
- **Wall thickness** is how thick the plastic is. The standard gives a minimum and a maximum.
- **Weight (kg per metre)** is how much one metre of that pipe weighs. Multiply by the length to get the weight of your order.

## How a pipe is named

Under IS 4984 a pipe is named in this order: **grade, PN, size (DN), SDR**. For example, **PE 80, PN 5, DN 200, SDR 26** means a PE80 pipe, rated 5 bar, 200 mm outside diameter, with the wall that goes with SDR 26. If a supplier writes it this way, you can read all four things at a glance.

## Step 1: What do PE63, PE80 and PE100 mean?

HDPE (high density polyethylene) pipes are made from different grades of plastic. The grade is the number after "PE". It comes from the plastic's long-term strength (called MRS), measured in megapascals.

| Grade | Strength (MRS) | Safe working stress |
|---|---|---|
| PE63 | 6.3 MPa | 5 MPa |
| PE80 | 8 MPa | 6.3 MPa |
| PE100 | 10 MPa | 8 MPa |

**In simple words:** a stronger plastic can hold the same pressure with a thinner wall. A PE100 pipe at PN 10 has a thinner wall than a PE80 pipe at PN 10, so it uses less plastic and weighs less.

## Step 2: What is PN?

PN stands for *nominal pressure*: the pressure, in **bar**, that the pipe is rated to carry continuously with water at 27 °C. HDPE pipe is designed for a 50-year working life at its rated pressure.

- 1 bar is roughly 10 metres of water height.
- A low PN (such as PN 4 or PN 6) is a lighter pipe for low-pressure lines.
- A middle PN (such as PN 8 or PN 10) suits many water supply and irrigation lines.
- A high PN (PN 16 and above) is for high-pressure lines.

The pressure ratings in the standard are: PN 2, 2.5, 3.2, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12.5, 16 and 20.

## Step 3: What is SDR?

SDR is the **Standard Dimension Ratio**: the pipe's outside diameter divided by its wall thickness.

> **SDR = outside diameter ÷ wall thickness**

Example: a pipe with a 110 mm outside diameter and an 8.1 mm wall has SDR = 110 ÷ 8.1 ≈ 13.6.

- **Small SDR (like 6 or 7.4)** means a thick wall: heavy and high pressure.
- **Large SDR (like 33 or 41)** means a thin wall: light and low pressure.

The standard uses ten SDR values: 41, 33, 26, 21, 17, 13.6, 11, 9, 7.4 and 6.

### How grade, PN and SDR fit together

Pick any two of grade, PN and SDR and the third is decided. The standard gives the formula:

> **Pressure (bar) = 10 × 2 × stress ÷ (SDR − 1) × 0.85**, rounded **down** to the nearest PN in the series.

(The 0.85 is a safety factor for water at 27 °C.)

Here is the full match-up from the standard:

| SDR | PE63 | PE80 | PE100 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 41 | PN 2 | PN 2.5 | PN 3.2 |
| 33 | PN 2.5 | PN 3.2 | PN 4 |
| 26 | PN 3.2 | PN 4 | PN 5 |
| 21 | PN 4 | PN 5 | PN 6 |
| 17 | PN 5 | PN 6 | PN 8 |
| 13.6 | PN 6 | PN 8 | PN 10 |
| 11 | PN 8 | PN 10 | PN 12.5 |
| 9 | not made | PN 12.5 | PN 16 |
| 7.4 | not made | PN 16 | PN 20 |
| 6 | not made | PN 20 | not made |

**Example:** you need a PN 10 pipe in PE100. Look in the PE100 column: PN 10 is SDR 13.6. For PE80, PN 10 is SDR 11, a thicker wall.

## Step 4: Wall thickness: why two numbers?

No pipe can be made to exactly one thickness, so the standard gives a **minimum** and a **maximum**. A pipe is acceptable when its wall is between the two everywhere.

The maximum is always the minimum plus a small allowance:

> **Maximum wall = minimum wall + (0.1 × minimum wall + 0.1 mm)**, rounded up to the next 0.1 mm.

For very small pipes the standard recommends a minimum wall of **2.0 mm**.

**Inside diameter (ID):** the hole the water flows through.

> **ID ≈ OD − 2 × wall thickness**

Using the *minimum* wall gives the largest possible ID. Because real pipes can have a slightly thicker wall, the actual hole may be a little smaller.

## Step 5: How is weight per metre worked out?

The weight in the chart is the weight of **one metre** of pipe. It is the volume of plastic in that metre multiplied by the density of the plastic (a little under 1 kg per litre, so HDPE floats on water).

> **kg per metre ≈ π × (OD − wall) × wall × density ÷ 1000** (OD and wall in mm, using the average of the minimum and maximum wall, density about 0.97 kg per litre)

**Note:** the standard itself lists sizes, pressure ratings and wall thicknesses, not weights. The weights in the chart below are reference weights worked out from those wall thicknesses. You do not need to do the sum yourself.

## How to read the chart

1. Find your **pipe size** (OD) in the left column.
2. Find the **PN** you need in the top row of your grade's table.
3. Where they meet you will see two things: the **weight in kg per metre** (bold) and the **wall thickness range** in mm.

### Worked example

You need **110 mm PE100 PN 10 pipe, 300 metres**.

1. In the PE100 table, row 110 mm, column PN 10 shows **2.667 kg/m**, wall **8.1–9.1 mm** (SDR 13.6).
2. Total weight = 2.667 × 300 = **about 800 kg**.
3. Inside diameter ≈ 110 − 2 × 8.1 = **93.8 mm**.

## Which grade should I choose?

- **Same pressure, less plastic:** a higher grade (PE100) uses a thinner wall than PE80 or PE63 for the same PN, so it is lighter per metre.
- **Choose the PN from the pressure your line really needs**, including pressure surges, not only the normal working pressure.

If you are unsure, tell us the pressure, the length and the size, and we will help you choose.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the chart weight the exact weight of every pipe?**
No. It is a reference weight. Because the wall is allowed to vary between the minimum and maximum, the real weight of a pipe can be slightly different. Our quotation shows the weight we will supply.

**Why is a PE100 pipe lighter than a PE63 pipe at the same pressure?**
The stronger plastic needs less wall to hold the same pressure.

**What does "IS 4984" mean?**
It is the Indian Standard for polyethylene pipes for water supply. This guide follows IS 4984:2016 as changed by Amendment No. 2 (April 2022).

**What changed in Amendment 2?**
The wall-thickness table was replaced (many maximums moved up by 0.1 mm, and the smallest pipes now have a 2.0 mm minimum), PE100 SDR 41 is now rated PN 3.2 instead of PN 3, and 2250 mm and 2500 mm sizes were added.

**Can I use the weight to compare quotes?**
Yes. Pipe is usually priced by weight, so compare the same size, grade and PN across suppliers. A thinner wall means less material and a lower price, but it may mean a lower pressure rating.

**Where can I read the standard?**
IS 4984 is published by the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS). You can get the standard and Amendment No. 2 from BIS.

Tell us the **size, grade (PE63/PE80/PE100), PN and length** you need and we will send you a quotation with the weight worked out for you.
